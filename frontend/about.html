// ARCHITECTURE & METHODOLOGY

How Aegis Investigates Claims

Investigate a claim. See the evidence. Understand the uncertainty. Aegis grounds fact-checking in multi-source retrieval, source lineage tracking, and explicit confidence scores rather than ungrounded model outputs.

INVESTIGATION PIPELINE 5-STEP EMPIRICAL VERIFICATION PROCESS
STEP 01

Normalize the Claim

Cleans input text, strips prompt-injection payloads, extracts core named entities, and computes an idempotency hash to prevent duplicate workloads.

STEP 02

Retrieve Evidence

Formulates targeted search queries across public web sources, news archives, and primary registries (SEC, PubMed, government bureaus).

STEP 03

Compare Sources

Identifies syndicated wire bulletins (collapsing replicated articles to their origin) and isolates direct contradictions between opposing reports.

STEP 04

Assess Evidence Quality

Ranks source authority, domain independence, and evidence saturation to distinguish genuine corroboration from echo-chamber repeats.

STEP 05

Synthesize Assessment

Produces a structured verdict with calibrated confidence bounds, key findings, unresolved gaps, and a verifiable evidence chain of custody.

TIER 2 • RESEARCH METHODOLOGY MATHEMATICAL & STATISTICAL INSTRUMENTS

Research & Simulation Methods

// STATISTICAL TEXT ANALYSIS

Zipf-Mandelbrot Regression

Measures lexical entropy and token frequency decay ($P(r) = P_0(r+\beta)^{-\gamma}$). Autoregressive language models sample with bounded variance and high fit ($R^2 > 0.93$), whereas human writing exhibits natural burstiness and lexical dispersion.

// INFORMATION DIFFUSION DYNAMICS

Hawkes Point-Process Modeling

Models narrative propagation as self-exciting point processes ($\lambda(t) = \mu + \sum_{t_i < t} \alpha e^{-\beta(t - t_i)}$). Calculates the reproduction number ($R_0 = \alpha / \beta$) to determine whether a claim is diffusing super-critically across networks.

// FAULT-TOLERANT AGGREGATION

Weighted-MSR Outlier Pruning

Derived from the Weighted Mean-Subsequence-Reduced algorithm in distributed systems. Eliminates the extreme highest and lowest evaluator scores before computing consensus, preventing hallucinating or sycophantic outlier evaluations from skewing the verdict.
